# Access diagnostics reference

## Manual offline workflow

1. Identify the service, reported impact, original vantage, incident window, reference time, and the supplied evidence bundles. Mark missing scope and provenance explicitly.
2. Establish source and collection provenance for each record. Use source-qualified aliases only with an explicit operator binding to the selected service and resource. An alias match, hostname, or similar label across runs does not prove identity.
3. Check collection age and event age independently at the incident reference time. Require known clock quality and uncertainty before ordering events. Preserve counter reset, wrap, sampling interval, direction, unit, and denominator as supplied. A relevant reset, wrap, or unknown counter state/semantics between samples blocks comparison across that discontinuity; do not skip it and compare outer values. Record `counter_discontinuity` with the applicable reset/unknown-semantics gap.
4. Compare only compatible observations in the declared window and mapping. For a bidirectional operation, count reverse coverage only when supported, nonmissing observations match the metric/operation, source/native identity, mapped resource, compatible measurement fields, and comparable event-time uncertainty in opposite explicit directions. Keep evidence for different layers separate: optical and PON state, subscriber authentication, DHCP, service VLAN, forwarding, Wi-Fi, and application evidence do not substitute for one another.
5. State observations before hypotheses. Give each finding the narrowest supported layer, run-qualified support, actual counterevidence, and explicit limitations. Preserve contradictory same-scope optical observations as actual counterevidence; report an `optical_conflict` with a conflict gap, and do not use a conflicted sample to assert degradation or normal optics. Name an observed resource only when the evidence and contract permit it; a mapped measurement resource alone does not identify a faulty device.
6. When evidence is stale, unbound, conflicting, incomparable, or absent, preserve the gap and withhold the narrower conclusion. A positive observation does not establish the next layer's outcome; an empty fault list does not establish health or recovery.

## Observation boundaries

Use only explicitly allowlisted typed records from the supplied carrier evidence. The supported vocabulary may include optical power and cited profile limits; LOS/LOF and PON session state; FEC/CRC counters; PPPoE, IPoE, or AAA outcomes; DHCP lease or pool state; expected/actual service VLAN; and forwarding, application, or Wi-Fi state. Values are source observations, not universal vendor semantics. Unknown metrics remain unused and visible as a coverage gap; do not translate command text, vendor phrases, or free-form values into a diagnosis.

An optical profile is applicable only when its cited limit and observed device-model evidence match the mapped resource, direction, unit, and validity interval. Do not invent thresholds or infer a model from a label. If a supported profile is unknown or its evidence is absent, report the missing profile input and leave the profile-dependent conclusion unsupported. A PON flap is an observed, strictly ordered up/down/up or down/up/down session sequence in comparable scope; it does not prove physical fiber damage or optical cause. Counter trends require comparable ordered cumulative samples with known interval, count, unit, denominator, and reset semantics. A relevant intermediate reset, wrap, or unknown counter state/semantics prevents a delta across that discontinuity. Any definitely time-ordered decrease among compatible cumulative samples inside the candidate interval also blocks that outer delta even when rows say `reset_state: none`; report `counter_discontinuity` without guessing the cause and retain other pairs that do not span it. This counter check is shared with transport analysis. Keep gaps distinct: unknown counter meaning, known non-cumulative semantics, missing sample count, missing interval, unit or denominator, unknown reset state, and reset/wrap each limit comparison. A positive counter increment alone is neutral evidence, not proof of fault or service impact. Authentication rejection is evidence about authentication only. DHCP failure, pool exhaustion, VLAN mismatch, forwarding failure, Wi-Fi failure, and application failure must each be supported at their own scope.

For provider-based optical conflict handling, only compare fresh readings of the same metric and bound resource with the same source/native alias, known equal unit and direction, matching denominator and interval, equal positive sample counts, known gauge semantics with no reset, overlapping event-time uncertainty, and unequal numeric values. The provider applies no guessed tolerance. It reports a neutral `optical_conflict` with actual counterevidence and a `conflicting_observations` gap, including when no profile is supplied. Either conflicted sample is withheld from degradation and normal-optics counterevidence; the conflict alone leaves status `insufficient_evidence`.

## Optional incident-owned provider

When an installed provider is discoverable through the host's supported mechanism and its Python runtime is available, inspect its bundled `references/access-analysis.md` before use. Do not assume a sibling Skill path or construct paths based on this Skill's location. The provider accepts an explicit JSON request, verifies supplied run hashes and M1/M2 references, recomputes freshness at the request's reference time, and emits deterministic JSON without changing a case. The provider does not collect from devices, access a network, import a run, or decide that the service recovered.

If the provider is missing or incompatible, continue manually when the supplied records are sufficient. Otherwise report the precise missing inputs or capability. Do not install dependencies, substitute a live probe, or treat a provider error as evidence about network state.

The [DHCP protocol overview](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc2131/), [PPPoE specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc2516/), and [RADIUS authentication protocol](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc2865/) are terminology references only. They do not define vendor metric semantics, prove a deployed implementation's behavior, or supply optical limits.

## Reporting

For each supported conclusion, record the observed fact, scoped hypothesis, source and timestamp, evidence IDs, mapped service/resource, direction and comparison basis, counterevidence, and remaining unknowns. Preserve actual status and time values; do not label an incident recovered without the agreed service criterion and independent verification over its required stability window. Do not perform or recommend an operational change as though it were already authorized or completed.
